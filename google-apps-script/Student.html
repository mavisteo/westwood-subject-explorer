Subject options: Frequently asked questions

Click a question to read the answer.

1. Is Pure Science better than Combined Science?

Neither is automatically better for you. Pure Science covers each science in greater breadth and depth. There is more content to learn, and you will encounter more demanding questions. You should therefore be prepared for a more rigorous course and a greater commitment of time and effort.

Choosing Combined Science does not mean that you cannot take H2 Science in JC. Your eligibility will depend on your results and the JC’s subject requirements.

2. Do I need Additional Mathematics or Pure Science to enter JC?

Additional Mathematics and Pure Science are not compulsory for general JC admission. However, qualifying for JC admission and qualifying for particular H2 subjects are different matters.

H2 Mathematics commonly requires an Additional Mathematics background, while H2 Sciences have their own requirements. Check the requirements for the JC subjects you may want to take before choosing your secondary subjects.

3. What should I consider when choosing my subject options?

Consider your interests, strengths, future plans and workload. Ask yourself: Do I enjoy this subject? What do my work, results and teachers’ feedback show? Does a pathway I am considering require particular subjects? Can I manage the workload alongside my other commitments?

Discuss your choices with your parents, teachers or ECG counsellor.

4. Can I take H2 Science in JC if I study Combined Science?

Yes, it may be possible, depending on the JC’s requirements and your results. Some JCs accept relevant Combined Science qualifications.

However, H2 Science builds on knowledge covered in Pure Science, so you may need to catch up on additional content.

5. Can I take Additional Mathematics without Pure Science?

Yes, if the school offers that combination and you meet its criteria. Additional Mathematics and Pure Science do not always have to be taken together. Refer to Westwood’s available combinations.

6. Do I need Pure Science for a science or engineering course at a polytechnic?

Pure Science is not a universal requirement. Relevant Combined Science subjects can meet the requirements for various courses. Check each course’s accepted subjects and minimum grades, as requirements differ.

7. Must I take Pure Humanities to enter JC?

No. Pure Humanities is not compulsory for general JC admission. It provides broader preparation in that discipline, but you should choose it because it suits your interests and strengths. Check individual JC requirements if you hope to continue a particular Humanities subject.

8. Is taking more subjects always better?

No. Additional subjects can broaden your learning, but they also add lessons, assignments and revision. Take an extra subject when you have a clear interest and can manage the workload. More subjects do not automatically lead to better results or more suitable pathways.

9. Are coursework subjects such as Art easier options?

Do not assume they are easier. Coursework requires sustained effort, planning and time beyond individual lessons. For example, students considering Art should be willing to develop ideas and work consistently on their coursework. Ask the subject teacher about the tasks and deadlines.

10. Should I aim to take subjects at a more demanding level?

Not necessarily. The most suitable subject level matches your readiness, learning pace and ability to perform well.

A student who achieves strong results at a less demanding level—for example, 65% or above—may be better placed to progress than one who struggles to pass or scores around 50% at a more demanding level. However, post-secondary eligibility also depends on the required subject levels and examination grades; these school marks are examples, not admission cut-offs.

Students learn at different paces. An appropriate subject level gives you time to strengthen your foundations, develop effective study skills and build confidence for your next stage of education.

The goal is to choose the level that helps you learn, grow and progress successfully.

11. Will I definitely receive my first-choice combination?

A first choice is a preference, not a guarantee. Allocation can depend on subject criteria, results, demand and available resources. Follow Westwood’s instructions and choose alternatives you would also be willing to study.

12. Can I change or drop a subject if I struggle in Secondary 3?

If you do not meet the academic requirements in Secondary 3, the school may ask you to take certain subjects at a more suitable level or drop one or more subjects. These changes may affect the post-secondary pathways available to you.

Speak to your subject teacher and Form Teacher early to discuss the support available and the implications of any change.

13. As a G2 student, can I drop my coursework subject and take only five subjects?

Yes, but you must speak to your Form Teacher first to discuss whether this is suitable for you.

Taking only five subjects can affect your post-secondary options. You will need to do well in all five subjects, as you will have fewer subjects to draw on when meeting entry requirements.

Consider your overall workload and how you can balance your time and effort across all your subjects. Discuss whether a lighter subject load would help you manage your learning and perform better.

Build your confidence

01

Before the interview

  • Read about the course: what will you learn, and why does it interest you?
  • Choose two or three real examples from your CCA, VIA, ApLM, projects or hobbies.
  • Review your application and portfolio. Be ready to explain everything you included.
  • Check the time, venue, documents and any online interview arrangements.
02

During the interview

  • Listen carefully. It is okay to pause and think or ask for a question to be repeated.
  • Answer the question directly, then support your point with an example.
  • Explain your own contribution and what you learnt.
  • Be honest about what you know. Speak clearly and respectfully.
03

After the interview

  • Thank the interviewers for their time.
  • Reflect on one thing you did well and one thing to improve.
  • Keep checking the official portal for next steps and deadlines.

A clearer version:

“I am interested in Early Childhood Education because I enjoy helping children learn. During VIA, I helped organise reading activities for children. I learnt to explain things patiently and adjust my approach when they needed more support.”

Use your own experiences and wording. Keep only statements that are true for you.

Keep practice respectful and focused on interviews. Avoid sharing phone numbers, addresses, identification numbers or other private details.

Tell your story with STAR

Use this as a guide when a question asks about an experience. Keep the answer natural.

S

Situation

What was happening? Give just enough context.

T

Task

What were you responsible for?

A

Action

What did you do, and why? Focus on your own contribution.

R

Result & reflection

What happened? What did you learn, and how would you improve?

Do not invent achievements or results. If an experience did not go as planned, explain honestly what you learnt and what you would do differently.

Questions to think about

Open a question for a few prompts to help you prepare.

Tell us about yourself.

Choose a few interests or strengths relevant to the course. Include a brief example rather than listing every achievement.

Why are you interested in this course?

Describe what interests you, what you have done to explore it and what you hope to learn. Be specific about the course.

Tell us about a challenge you faced.

Explain the situation, your response and what changed. Include what you learnt, even if the outcome was not perfect.

How have you contributed to a team?

Share your role and one concrete action. How did you work with others or respond to a disagreement?

Show us something from your portfolio.

Explain why you chose the item, your own contribution and your learning. Be ready to describe your thinking process.

Do you have any questions for us?

Prepare a thoughtful question about learning experiences, projects or student life. Ask something you would like to understand better.

These are general practice questions. Actual interview questions and selection tasks depend on the institution and course.
